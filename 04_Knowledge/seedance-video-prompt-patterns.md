# Seedance Video Prompt Patterns for Credible Local-Business Content

## Purpose

This note distils a small, original set of reusable video-generation and editing patterns from the public Dreamina Seedance 2.5 guide. It is a working reference for Sahas AI social creative, not proof of a vendor capability, a client outcome, or a publish-ready workflow.

Use the vendor UI and current terms to verify supported durations, formats, input limits, and commercial rights before a paid client workflow.

## Operating boundaries

- Use supplied real business photos, products, locations, and verified evidence where trust matters.
- Generate text-free visuals, then add essential English, Hindi, or Marathi copy in Canva for accuracy and editability.
- Never fabricate testimonials, metrics, reviews, awards, staff, students, patients, premises, product features, or outcomes.
- Preserve supplied identities, logo details, product shape, and location. Do not represent generated people or scenes as a real client.
- Every public asset requires Yogesh/client approval before publication, per [[social-media-automation-offer-plan]] (emerging).

## Reusable prompt patterns

### 1. Clean marketing B-roll

Use when the final Reel/post needs a credible text-free visual layer.

```text
Create a [6-10 second] [9:16 or 4:5] video of [real business/product/location].
Show [one simple action] with [specific camera movement].
Keep the visual natural, credible, and suitable for a local-business marketing post.
Preserve [subject/product/location] exactly as supplied.
Pure video: no subtitles, no on-screen text, no background music, no voice-over,
no watermark, no invented logo, and no extra people.
```

### 2. Continue a finished short clip

Describe only the new segment, beginning from the last frame.

```text
Continue directly from the final frame.
For the next [4-6 seconds], [subject] [one clear action].
Maintain the same identity, outfit, location, time of day, lighting, camera direction,
and visual style. End with [clear final pose/frame].
No new text, music, logo, or unrelated object.
```

### 3. Change one marked element

Use only after marking the exact target element in the video editor.

```text
Change only the marked [object/area] to [required change] throughout the entire video.
Keep the subject, face, body, product shape, background, composition, camera movement,
lighting, shadows, reflections, timing, and all other elements unchanged.
Maintain realistic texture and consistency in every frame.
```

### 4. Maintain consistency across cuts

```text
Use the supplied reference assets as the source of truth.
Keep the same [person/product/business location] consistent across every shot:
same identity, facial features, proportions, clothing/product details, colours, and environment.
Create [number] short shots showing [shot list].
Maintain continuous lighting, direction of movement, spatial layout, and camera language.
Use natural skin texture, realistic materials, and physically believable motion.
No duplicated subject, face drift, costume changes, or background swaps.
```

### 5. Plan a Reel as a storyboard first

```text
Create a [6-shot] vertical 9:16 storyboard for a [local-business type] Reel.
Goal: [one credible message or action].
Shot 1: [establishing real location/product].
Shot 2: [problem or enquiry moment].
Shot 3: [service/action].
Shot 4: [benefit shown without inventing a result].
Shot 5: [human/business proof using supplied real asset].
Shot 6: [clean closing visual with empty space for Canva CTA].
Keep the same location, subject identity, lighting direction, and colour palette throughout.
No on-screen text, no watermark, no fake dashboard, no fabricated testimonial or metric.
```

## Validation required before promotion

Test each pattern on two or three real Sahas AI assets. Record generation attempts, edit time, identity consistency, text accuracy, approval outcome, cost, and whether the output can truthfully represent the business. Only then consider a vendor-neutral SOP and promote maturity.
