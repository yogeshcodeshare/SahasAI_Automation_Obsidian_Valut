# Lead Capture — Showcase-First vs Requirement-First

The trainer's claim: every WhatsApp automation flow, in any industry, is structured one of exactly two ways. Named as "Option 1" and "Option 2" in the class; renamed here for clarity.

## Option 1 — showcase first, capture second

The retail-shop analogy the trainer uses: you don't walk into a shop and announce your exact requirement before looking around. The shop shows you what it has; you browse; your selection **is** the requirement, captured as a byproduct of you choosing.

**Mechanically:** show a menu of offerings → the lead selects → that selection gets written to a tag/attribute → a booking or requirement form collects whatever's left.

**Fits when:** the option count is small enough to browse practically. The trainer's own example: a travel business with 20–50 fixed packages.

## Option 2 — capture first, filter and showcase second

The reverse order: ask the lead's requirement before showing anything, then filter your own backend inventory against that answer and show only what matches.

**Mechanically:** ask a requirement question → the answer filters the backend project/product list → only the matching subset gets shown, rather than the full catalog.

**Fits when:** there are too many options to browse, or — the trainer's phrasing — the lead "doesn't even know what you offer." Named in the class, not built as a worked flow.

## The deciding question

> Can the lead sensibly browse everything you have, or is the catalog too large / too undifferentiated for that to work?

Small, browsable catalog → Option 1. Large or unclear catalog → Option 2.

## Why this belongs in a discovery call

This is a structural decision, not a preference to change mid-build — the trainer is explicit it should be decided before any flow gets built. Asking a prospective client *"do your customers already know what they want, or do they need to be shown first?"* identifies which structure to design before any technical work starts, and prevents rebuilding a flow around the wrong assumption partway through a project.
