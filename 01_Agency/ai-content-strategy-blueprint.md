# AI Content Strategy Blueprint — Sahas AI

**Status:** Working strategy and topic backlog
**Primary channels:** Instagram Reels; adapt selected ideas for LinkedIn when useful
**Language:** Conversational Marathi-first, with familiar English business and technology terms
**Audience hypothesis:** Coaching-class owners first; later test topics with institute owners, service providers, and other business owners

> This is an adapted planning document based on the user-supplied 35-video strategy. It is not a verbatim copy or an approved publishing schedule. The existing `Sahas AI content calendar.md` remains the plan for the first 30 days; this blueprint is a longer-term idea bank. The first three Sahas AI introduction videos remain separate from that calendar.

## Sahas AI's current stage

- WhatsApp message templates are being prepared; a complete WhatsApp automation workflow is not ready for public demonstration.
- n8n, AI agents, and AI voice agents are learning and future-building areas—not capabilities to present as deployed or proven.
- Early content should explain business problems and responsible automation in plain language. Use build demonstrations only after a working version has been tested and approved to show.
- Every topic below is an idea, not a claim that Sahas AI has already delivered the result.

## How to use this blueprint

- Each entry gives a **Name**, one short **Hook (0–7s)**, a modest **Promise**, a proposed **Format**, and **Why it's better** for the intended audience.
- Hooks are opening-line options only; these are not complete scripts, workflows, legal advice, or implementation guides.
- Advanced and time-sensitive ideas are marked for a later stage. Check current official sources and test the relevant capability before publishing specifics.
- Any scenario without a real, permissioned, documented client example must be identified as hypothetical.

## Category 1 — Beginner clarity and common mistakes

### 1. Understand the costs before choosing a WhatsApp API setup — Verify first

- **Hook (0–7s):** “WhatsApp API setup ठरवण्याआधी, एकूण खर्चात कोणते घटक येतात हे तपासलंय का?”
- **Promise:** Help viewers make a list of cost items to verify in current official information and provider quotes.
- **Format:** Plain-language checklist; no exact price claims.
- **Why it's better:** Encourages informed budgeting instead of repeating stale prices or promising a fixed bill.

### 2. Ask who controls the messaging account and number

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Setupची चर्चा सुरू आहे—account आणि numberचा control नेमका कोणाकडे राहणार?”
- **Promise:** Give viewers practical questions to ask a provider before committing.
- **Format:** Founder-to-camera question list; verify current product terminology first.
- **Why it's better:** Focuses on ownership and access questions without making unsupported claims about any provider.

### 3. More messages do not automatically mean better communication

- **Hook (0–7s):** “जास्त messages पाठवणं म्हणजे चांगली customer communication strategy असतेच का?”
- **Promise:** Explain why relevance, permission, and customer expectations matter when planning outreach.
- **Format:** Myth-versus-question text card; confirm current platform requirements before discussing rules.
- **Why it's better:** Replaces volume promises with a customer-respect perspective.

### 4. Button-based replies and AI agents are not interchangeable terms

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Button निवडणारा chatbot आणि AI agent—दोन्हींचं काम खरंच सारखं असतं का?”
- **Promise:** Introduce the conceptual distinction between fixed choices and systems that interpret more open-ended requests.
- **Format:** Simple side-by-side concept graphic; no product demo.
- **Why it's better:** Sets clearer expectations without presenting an untested Sahas AI capability.

### 5. Organize the process before changing the CRM

- **Hook (0–7s):** “CRM बदलण्याआधी, enquiryनंतरची पुढची पायरी स्पष्ट आहे का?”
- **Promise:** Show why teams should agree on the process and information they need before choosing software.
- **Format:** Paper sketch or founder explanation; no data migration tutorial.
- **Why it's better:** Addresses the underlying work instead of implying that buying a tool fixes it.

### 6. Complaints need thoughtful human attention

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Complaint आली तर लगेच generic reply पाठवणं योग्य ठरेल का?”
- **Promise:** Explain why sensitive or unusual situations deserve human judgment.
- **Format:** Talking head with an illustrative scenario; no claim of automated sentiment detection.
- **Why it's better:** Builds trust by acknowledging where a person should remain involved.

### 7. Plan customer permission and privacy before outreach

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Customerला message पाठवण्याआधी, त्याला तो message अपेक्षित आहे का?”
- **Promise:** Introduce permission and privacy as design questions for customer communication.
- **Format:** Short explainer; verify current official Meta and applicable legal guidance before stating requirements.
- **Why it's better:** Treats consent as a trust issue and avoids unsupported legal assurances.

### 8. Know what happens after a Click-to-WhatsApp ad

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Adवर click झालं—पण पुढे आलेली enquiry कुठून आली हे कसं कळणार?”
- **Promise:** Explain the general idea of connecting an ad interaction to a later business outcome.
- **Format:** Conceptual customer-journey graphic; detailed tracking only after a tested setup exists.
- **Why it's better:** Connects marketing activity to follow-up without promising a particular return.

### 9. A quote may need context, not just a fixed reply

- **Hook (0–7s):** “प्रत्येक enquiryला एकच fixed quote योग्य असतो का?”
- **Promise:** Show why businesses should define which details a person needs before preparing a quote.
- **Format:** Fictional example using clearly illustrative inputs; no tax or pricing advice.
- **Why it's better:** Focuses on accurate context rather than an untested instant-quote promise.

### 10. Choose customer communication spaces with privacy in mind

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Customerची माहिती groupमध्ये share करण्याआधी, कोणाला काय दिसतं हे तपासा.”
- **Promise:** Encourage owners to consider visibility, access, and customer expectations when choosing a communication format.
- **Format:** Privacy-awareness explainer; verify any platform-specific statements.
- **Why it's better:** Avoids blanket security claims and keeps the focus on thoughtful handling of information.

### 11. A workflow should have a way to notice when a step fails

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Form submit झाला—पण पुढची action झाली का, हे कसं कळेल?”
- **Promise:** Introduce testing and failure visibility as questions to ask before relying on a connected process.
- **Format:** Concept-only diagram; demonstrate only after a real, tested build exists.
- **Why it's better:** Goes beyond a happy-path demo without pretending a Sahas AI system is live.

### 12. Decide what success means before measuring it

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Automationचं success ठरवताना नेमकं काय मोजायचं?”
- **Promise:** Help viewers define a relevant outcome for their process before choosing metrics.
- **Format:** Talking head with a generic example; do not use unsupported benchmark figures.
- **Why it's better:** Avoids vanity metrics and unverified performance promises.

### 13. Make customer conversation ownership clear within a team

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Teamमधल्या प्रत्येकाला वाटत असेल की दुसराच reply देईल—मग?”
- **Promise:** Explain why teams need clarity about who owns the next response.
- **Format:** Illustrative role scenario; no shared-inbox feature claims.
- **Why it's better:** Addresses responsibility and continuity before promoting a platform.

### 14. Keep the first customer message clear and focused

- **Hook (0–7s):** “पहिल्याच messageमध्ये सगळी माहिती द्यावी लागते का?”
- **Promise:** Encourage clear, relevant messages with one understandable next step.
- **Format:** Before-and-after wording example, clearly labelled as a sample.
- **Why it's better:** Teaches clarity without claiming a specific reply rate or guaranteed result.

### 15. Understand what a verification badge does—and does not—mean

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Verification badge दिसला म्हणजे कोणत्या गोष्टीची खात्री मिळते?”
- **Promise:** Encourage viewers to distinguish platform verification from broader business quality or performance.
- **Format:** Short explainer; check current official eligibility and badge terminology before recording.
- **Why it's better:** Sets realistic expectations and avoids promising approval or status.

## Category 2 — Balanced, contrarian perspectives

### 16. AI does not have to replace the person who closes a sale

- **Hook (0–7s):** “AIचं काम प्रत्येक sale close करणं असायलाच हवं का?”
- **Promise:** Explore how AI may assist with bounded tasks while people handle judgment and important decisions.
- **Format:** Founder-to-camera opinion; keep it conceptual.
- **Why it's better:** Offers a human-first position instead of presenting replacement as the goal.

### 17. Manual follow-up still has a place

- **Hook (0–7s):** “प्रत्येक follow-up automate करायचा असतो का?”
- **Promise:** Explain why some customer situations call for a personal response and others may benefit from structure.
- **Format:** Two hypothetical situations, clearly labelled illustrative.
- **Why it's better:** Makes room for context instead of treating automation as the answer to every conversation.

### 18. A spreadsheet may not be the root cause

- **Hook (0–7s):** “नवीन software घेतलं—पण कामाची पद्धत अजूनही अस्पष्ट आहे का?”
- **Promise:** Show why teams should define ownership and next steps before switching tools.
- **Format:** Paper-versus-process analogy; no migration claims.
- **Why it's better:** Separates a process problem from a software preference.

### 19. Choose a customer channel based on the customer journey

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Customerला नवीन app द्यायचं की सध्याच्या channelवर मदत करायची?”
- **Promise:** Encourage owners to consider their audience and task before adding another channel.
- **Format:** Simple decision questions; avoid claiming that everyone prefers one channel.
- **Why it's better:** Starts with customer context rather than a one-size-fits-all technology pitch.

### 20. Not every team needs an API setup immediately

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Official API घ्यायच्या आधी, तुमची गरज आणि तयारी तपासली आहे का?”
- **Promise:** Introduce readiness questions such as process clarity, team needs, and budget ownership.
- **Format:** Talking head with a readiness checklist; verify current product options and terms.
- **Why it's better:** Helps viewers evaluate fit instead of pushing the same purchase on everyone.

## Category 3 — Tutorials to consider only after a working build

> These ideas are not for the current introductory phase. Publish only after Sahas AI has built, tested, and received approval to demonstrate the relevant setup.

### 21. From an enquiry form to a customer notification

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Formमध्ये enquiry आली—पुढची योग्य action कोणती असू शकते?”
- **Promise:** If a tested setup exists, show how information moves between the form and an approved next step.
- **Format:** Screen demonstration of a tested setup, with private details hidden.
- **Why it's better:** Teaches a real, verified example rather than claiming a universal instant setup.

### 22. Test a customer-message process before using it

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Message पाठवण्याआधी कोणत्या गोष्टी test करायला हव्यात?”
- **Promise:** Show a test checklist for accuracy, recipient, purpose, and expected next step.
- **Format:** Demonstration only after the message and setup are approved; verify current template rules.
- **Why it's better:** Prioritizes correctness and customer experience over a quick-launch claim.

### 23. Route sensitive situations to a person

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Systemला कधी थांबून माणसाकडे द्यायला हवं?”
- **Promise:** Once built and tested, demonstrate the human-review path for a defined situation.
- **Format:** Screen demo of a real test environment; never show private customer data.
- **Why it's better:** Makes the escalation boundary visible instead of promising that AI understands every situation.

### 24. Prepare a quote from verified inputs

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Quote तयार करताना कोणती माहिती confirm असणं गरजेचं आहे?”
- **Promise:** If a tested prototype exists, explain which inputs are used and where a person reviews the result.
- **Format:** Clearly labelled sample document demo; no tax or financial advice.
- **Why it's better:** Shows the review and accuracy requirements, not just the speed of generating a file.

### 25. Trace an ad enquiry to its next business step

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Adमधून आलेली enquiry पुढे कुठे जाते, हे track करता येतं का?”
- **Promise:** After a real setup exists, explain what the tracking can and cannot show.
- **Format:** Tested screen demo with account identifiers and customer details hidden.
- **Why it's better:** Connects reporting to a defined journey and avoids unverified return-on-ad-spend claims.

## Category 4 — Story-based ideas

> Until Sahas AI has a real, documented, permissioned case study, present these as fictional or illustrative scenarios—not customer results.

### 26. The enquiry that had no clear owner

- **Hook (0–7s):** “सगळ्यांना message दिसला—पण reply कोण देणार हे ठरलं नव्हतं.”
- **Promise:** Show how role clarity can matter in a hypothetical customer-service moment.
- **Format:** Short illustrative story, labelled hypothetical.
- **Why it's better:** Uses a relatable operational tension without inventing a client or outcome.

### 27. A question arrives after the class ends

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Class संपली—आणि त्यानंतर एक महत्त्वाची enquiry आली.”
- **Promise:** Explore what a thoughtful next step could look like when a person is unavailable.
- **Format:** Coaching-class scenario, explicitly illustrative; no response-time guarantee.
- **Why it's better:** Connects to the initial audience hypothesis without claiming an existing automation.

### 28. The owner tracking every follow-up

- **Hook (0–7s):** “प्रत्येक follow-up स्वतः लक्षात ठेवणं—हेच रोजचं काम झालंय का?”
- **Promise:** Help viewers recognize when work depends too heavily on one person remembering every step.
- **Format:** Empathetic, hypothetical founder-style narration; no invented biography or statistics.
- **Why it's better:** Focuses on the work pattern rather than a fabricated transformation story.

### 29. A fee question that needs more context

- **Hook (0–7s):** “एकच fee message प्रत्येक enquiryला योग्य answer देईल का?”
- **Promise:** Show why a person may need context before giving a useful answer.
- **Format:** Fictional dialogue summary or text scenario; no scripted customer quote presented as real.
- **Why it's better:** Highlights thoughtful communication instead of pushing an automatic sales reply.

### 30. Check the cost model before a surprise arrives

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Automation सुरू करण्याआधी खर्च कसा मोजला जाईल हे विचारलंय का?”
- **Promise:** Encourage a business to check pricing assumptions and usage terms before committing.
- **Format:** Budget-question checklist; use only current verified prices if naming any.
- **Why it's better:** Teaches preparation without inventing a dramatic invoice or penalty story.

## Category 5 — Proposed practical frameworks

> Names below are working descriptions, not established Sahas AI intellectual property. Test them with the audience before branding them as a formal framework.

### 31. A two-question task filter: repeatability and human judgment

- **Hook (0–7s):** “हे काम वारंवार होतं का—आणि यात मानवी judgment किती महत्त्वाचं आहे?”
- **Promise:** Offer two questions to start a discussion about whether a task is a sensible automation candidate.
- **Format:** Simple two-axis whiteboard concept; no score or universal rule.
- **Why it's better:** Makes the decision approachable without claiming that one matrix fits every business.

### 32. A follow-up readiness checklist

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Follow-up ठरवण्याआधी—context, owner आणि पुढची पायरी स्पष्ट आहेत का?”
- **Promise:** Help viewers identify information and ownership questions before selecting a reminder method.
- **Format:** Short checklist graphic, not a workflow specification.
- **Why it's better:** Focuses on clarity and customer intent instead of fixed timing promises.

### 33. A data-readiness checklist

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Automationला माहिती देण्याआधी ती योग्य आणि समजण्यासारखी आहे का?”
- **Promise:** Introduce basic questions about accuracy, purpose, access, and responsibility for information.
- **Format:** Four-point checklist; no migration tutorial.
- **Why it's better:** Encourages preparation without shaming a team's current tools.

### 34. Map the customer conversation before choosing a tool

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Customerचा प्रश्न, context, उत्तर आणि पुढची पायरी—हे आधी map केलंय का?”
- **Promise:** Explain why understanding the conversation can help inform later tool decisions.
- **Format:** Conceptual journey sketch only; not an implementation flow.
- **Why it's better:** Keeps the customer experience in view before technical choices are made.

### 35. A readiness check before publishing an automation

- **Hook (0–7s):** “Automation live करण्याआधी—test, approval आणि human handoff तपासलंय का?”
- **Promise:** Offer a high-level review lens for accuracy, ownership, testing, and human oversight.
- **Format:** Readiness checklist; verify applicable platform requirements before including policy details.
- **Why it's better:** Makes responsible review part of the story instead of promising a launch without risk.

## Editorial and evidence rules

- Do not invent client stories, testimonials, results, credentials, savings, revenue, or guarantees.
- Do not reuse competitor hooks, scripts, names for frameworks, or brand voice. Use only the general topic opportunity and create original wording.
- Treat every statistic, price, account limit, approval condition, legal statement, and performance result from the source as unverified until checked against a current primary source.
- For platform and privacy topics, verify current official information before publishing specifics; do not present the content as legal advice.
- Mark scenarios as hypothetical unless a real case is documented and the owner has permission to share it.
- Keep future capability topics separate from what Sahas AI has actually built and tested.
- Keep the existing 30-day calendar as the first publishing plan; choose from this backlog only after reviewing audience responses and readiness.
